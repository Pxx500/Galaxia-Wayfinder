# Audit Galaxia Concept Map

## Question

For every concept represented in the archived 37-page Galaxia Concept Map, which claims are already captured by resolved Wayfinder decisions, which must become new tickets or fog, which belong after Alpha, which are obsolete or disputed, and which implementation-status labels still match current code?

## Resolution

- Audit rule: the archived concept map was generated by an earlier agent with incomplete context and is not a design authority. Product claims require owner confirmation or corroboration by accepted Wayfinder decisions; implementation claims require direct code verification. Forced connective text may be discarded without creating a decision.
- Source priority rule: current owner decisions and accepted Wayfinder notes override imported material; other developer documents outrank this concept map; the concept map is a low-priority, potentially outdated gap inventory. Conflicts are resolved against the higher-priority source rather than presented as fresh decisions.
- `09.1 Teams, Roles & Permissions` and the role-based portion of `09.3 Collaborative Infrastructure Control` are rejected design. The configurable Galaxia role and permission system was added without an accepted product rationale and should not be treated as part of Alpha merely because its code exists.
- `09.2 Shared Asset Ownership` is retained: the GTNH team is the owner of shared Galaxia infrastructure and strategic assets.
- Every member of the owning GTNH team has equal access to its Galaxia assets. Galaxia adds no roles, ranks, or configurable permission layer above team membership.

## Verified Implementation Facts

- The `01.1 Celestial Object Hierarchy`, `01.2 Interactive Starmap`, and `01.3 Dynamic Asteroid Fields` foundations exist: the registry handles authored and dynamic celestial keys, generated asteroids can materialize into the hierarchy and Star Map projection, and the interactive orbital view exposes object and asset state. Their future actionability and physical-representation claims are already governed by Define Celestial Object and Gameplay Space Mapping, Require Star Map Object Actionability, Define Asteroid Belt Physical Travel and Beacons, and Define Asteroid Automation Contract.
- The `01.4 Orbital Navigation & Transfers` status is accurately partial: orbital route calculation and trajectory presentation exist, while authoritative crew and cargo journey contracts remain owned by Define Vehicle Capability and Route Requirements and Define Alpha Transport Roles.
- The `02.1 Modular Rocket Design` foundations exist: blueprints, registered parts, assembly analysis, build state, and editor interactions are implemented. Unimplemented mission-stat ambitions are not accepted from this source and remain governed by Define Vehicle Capability and Route Requirements and Define Flight Progression Balance Invariants.
- The `02.2 Construction & Launch` and `02.3 Physical Flight & Landing` labels are accurately partial: construction and launch foundations plus a physical rocket entity exist, but complete destination-aware journey and arrival behavior does not. Construction-cost communication is already routed to Issue - Add Construction Requirement Information Control, while the source's configurable multiplayer-permission language is rejected with `09.1`.
- The `03.1 Playable Worlds & Terrain`, `03.2 Environmental Hazards`, and `03.3 Survival Gear & Movement` foundations exist: playable dimension materialization, terrain and provider configuration, environmental state, hazard warnings and HUD, oxygen equipment, Baubles integration, gravity, and zero-G movement are present. Their Alpha completeness is evaluated per destination by Define Minimum Galacticraft-Equivalent Destination Contract, Define Destination Access Levels, and Define Destination Role Taxonomy, not by accepting the flowchart's generic depth promises.
- The `03.4 Manual Exploration Loop` correctly identifies that no single coherent arrival-to-survey-to-knowledge loop is currently established, but its proposed survey loop is not accepted merely because the flowchart states it. Manual presence and research roles remain routed to Define Destination Role Taxonomy and Define Alpha Research and Production Floor.
- The `04.1 Team Celestial Knowledge`, `04.2 Satellite Prospecting`, and `04.3 Discovery-Gated Visibility` foundations exist: team facts and discovery progress persist, scan workers aggregate into discovery tasks, and Star Map projection respects team knowledge. Alpha knowledge depth remains owned by Define Alpha Celestial Knowledge Layers rather than the flowchart's proposed multi-stage vocabulary.
- `04.4 Research Progression & Objectives` remains planned. Existing research-themed blocks do not establish a mission, point, unlock, or objective loop; any mandatory Alpha research chain belongs to Define Alpha Research and Production Floor.
- The `05.1 Team-Owned Satellite Assets` and `05.2 Communication Network` foundations exist: satellites are persistent team assets, and the server derives network topology, widest-path capacity, active use, and read-only client state.
- Current code gives the Prospecting Satellite two distinct responsibilities: it contributes workers to Celestial Discovery scans and also participates in the ongoing Raw Prospecting Data workflow.
- Accepted Alpha contract: one Prospecting Satellite performs both responsibilities. It contributes discovery work for eligible hidden objects within its detection scope and independently produces ongoing Raw Prospecting Data about the celestial object it orbits.
- Discovery progress and Raw Prospecting Data remain separate processes even though one satellite type supplies both. This decision does not determine whether the simpler opening payload from Define Alpha Observation Opening is itself a Prospecting Satellite; that remains part of Specify Alpha Opening Mission Flow.
- `05.3 Prospecting Satellite Role` is superseded by this accepted dual-role contract. The satellite remains a discovery-worker source but now also participates directly in gameplay data transport as a Raw Prospecting Data producer.
- The `06.1 Automated Facilities`, `06.2 Facility Modules`, and `06.3 Remote Management` foundations exist. Their generic future heat, utility-network, layout, and permission claims are not accepted from the flowchart; current Alpha decisions and open facility tickets remain authoritative.
- GTNHLib `0.11.33` does not allow a one-member team to be disbanded, but an owner may disband a team with more than one member. The old team is removed and every former member receives a new singleton team with a different Team ID.
- Leaving a multi-member team creates a new singleton team for the departing player. GTNHLib rejects leaving as the final member and rejects departure of the final owner, so a team is not emptied through the normal leave command.
- A merge consumes one Team ID and preserves the surviving Team ID.
- Galaxia currently handles `TeamMergeEvent` and transfers assets from the consumed team. GTNHLib exposes no corresponding disband/removal event, and Galaxia's team-removal path is reachable only from its merge handler.
- Therefore the `09.2 Shared Asset Ownership` source claim that assets are removed when a team is deleted does not match current behavior. Disbanding or emptying a team can leave Galaxia state associated with a Team ID that no longer exists.
- During a normal `/team disband`, GTNHLib still knows the initiating player's UUID and verifies that the player is an owner before removing the team. A team may have multiple owners.
- GTNHLib currently discards that initiator context before Galaxia can observe it: normal disband calls `TeamActions.onDisband(team, false, null)`, and no disband event is published. Galaxia therefore cannot currently identify the initiating owner or the owner's newly created singleton Team ID.
- Transferring shared assets to the initiating owner's replacement team is technically possible if GTNHLib exposes a post-disband event containing the removed Team ID, initiating owner UUID, and replacement Team ID. Administrator-forced disband remains a separate case because its command sender need not be a team owner.
- Accepted behavior for normal owner-initiated disband: all Galaxia assets owned by the removed team transfer to the newly created singleton team of the owner who executed the disband command. They are neither deleted nor duplicated among former members.
- Accepted behavior for administrator-forced disband: the administrator must either identify an owner whose replacement team receives all Galaxia assets or pass an explicit destructive argument authorizing their deletion. Administrative disband without either choice is rejected.
- Implementation follow-up is routed to Issue - Preserve Assets Across Team Disband.
- GTNHLib also rejects administrative disband of a singleton team. The asset-successor rules therefore apply to removed multi-member teams, while singleton teams remain stable under normal team commands.
- `05.4 Gameplay Data Transport` is retained as an Alpha mechanic only if Alpha contains at least one real, player-useful producer-to-consumer data flow whose behavior is constrained by satellite-network connectivity or capacity.
- A debug-only generator or visualization does not satisfy this requirement. Exposing the transport system without a real gameplay benefit would create an orphaned mechanic and violate the Alpha destination.
- Prospecting satellites produce actionable local knowledge about the celestial object they orbit. Their accepted gameplay effect is increased extraction efficiency for mining performed on that same celestial object.
- Prospecting is an explicit round-trip data workflow: a Prospecting Satellite generates `Raw Prospecting Data`; the communication network transports it to a processing capability; processing produces `Processed Prospecting Data`; and the network returns the processed result to the originating celestial object.
- Mining on that object receives the prospecting-efficiency benefit only from processed results that have completed the return path. Satellite presence alone does not grant the bonus.
- The exact efficiency function, stacking behavior, processing location, data volume, and result lifetime remain open.
- The processing capability itself, including whether it is a computer, Data Processor, facility module, or another concept, is delegated to Define Prospecting Data Processing.
- Every Prospecting Satellite produces its own independent Raw Prospecting Data stream.
- The extraction-efficiency bonus is determined by the aggregate amount of data that has successfully completed processing and returned to the originating celestial object, not directly by the number of deployed satellites.
- Satellite count, communication capacity, and processing throughput affect the bonus only by changing how much processed data reaches the object.
- Processed Prospecting Data is an ongoing consumable rather than permanent cumulative knowledge. Sustaining an extraction-efficiency bonus continuously consumes the processed-data supply returned to the object.
- The object evaluates consumed or available processed-data throughput over a short discrete window, caches the resulting extraction bonus, and exposes that stable value during the following window. Per-tick extraction does not require per-tick network or bonus recomputation.
- A 20-tick window is a reasonable initial tuning value, not a durable Wayfinder requirement. Smooth visual interpolation may be added at presentation boundaries if discrete bonus changes are noticeable.
- Each Prospecting Satellite produces a fixed amount of Raw Prospecting Data per tick. Mining activity and extractor count do not change satellite production.
- The originating celestial object consumes returned Processed Prospecting Data at a fixed rate rather than in proportion to mining throughput. The sustained returned-data rate determines one object-wide extraction-efficiency bonus shared by all mining on that object.
- Adding miners does not increase prospecting-data demand. Additional satellites, processing, and communication capacity improve the shared bonus by increasing the processed-data rate that can be sustained and returned.
- Working Alpha balance: one fully processed and returned data stream equal to one Prospecting Satellite's fixed production rate grants all miners on the originating celestial object `+10%` operating speed and `+5 percentage points` chance to double the extracted ore output.
- Bonus contribution scales proportionally for partial sustained streams and stacks linearly across multiple full streams. These numeric values are provisional balance tuning rather than stable product constants.
- The output bonus has no cap and is represented as an expected-output multiplier rather than a bounded doubling chance: `outputMultiplier = 1 + 0.05 * sustainedSatelliteEquivalentStreams`.
- Each extraction produces `floor(outputMultiplier)` guaranteed output batches and one additional batch with probability equal to the multiplier's fractional part. For example, `2.25` guarantees double output and has a `25%` chance to produce triple output.
- The corresponding working speed formula is `speedMultiplier = 1 + 0.10 * sustainedSatelliteEquivalentStreams`.
- Each Prospecting Satellite has a bounded Raw Prospecting Data buffer. Once full, it cannot continue accumulating scan output until data leaves the buffer.
- Prospecting represents an ongoing scan and computation pipeline, not stored permanent survey progress. Raw production, network transfer, processing, and the return path must continue operating to sustain the planet-wide bonus.
- Returned processed data does not form an unlimited long-term bank. Only the short evaluation state needed to calculate and cache the current bonus is retained, so end-to-end pipeline uptime remains meaningful.
- Prospecting benefits are intended to cover every Galaxia extraction system and an integration path for relevant GT5 extraction machines.
- The working `+10%` speed and `+5%` expected-output values are a generic baseline, not a decision that every machine receives identical effects. Frackers, Automated Facility miners, physical drills, GT5 machines, and future extraction systems may respond differently.
- Machine-specific prospecting behavior is deferred to Define Prospecting Effects by Extraction System.
- `02.4 Advanced Freight & Shipyard` is provisionally retained as an Alpha requirement rather than classified as post-Alpha scope. Cargo ships and orbital shipyards are expected to participate in the complete Ore Tier 1-10 progression unless later route mapping proves they are unnecessary.
- Their minimum Alpha role and the capability gap they fill beyond modular rockets, HAMMER, and ordinary deliveries remain delegated to Define Alpha Transport Roles and Define Vehicle Capability and Route Requirements.
- Accepted working transport roles: reusable cargo ships provide high-volume space-to-space freight between locations without a major gravity-well ascent; orbital shipyards build, dock, refuel, and transfer cargo for those ships; HAMMER handles cargo logistics involving airless surfaces or other objects without atmosphere.
- Whether rockets should be restricted exclusively to atmosphere-bearing planets remains open. The broader boundary between atmosphere, gravity-well access, HAMMER eligibility, and rocket necessity belongs to Define Alpha Transport Roles.
- Route access and transport cost are derived from multiple relevant endpoint, environment, vehicle, and payload variables rather than from atmosphere alone or a hardcoded destination whitelist.
- Every derived cost or failed capability check must be explainable before commitment through a player-readable breakdown showing which factors contributed and which vehicle requirement is unmet. A mathematically derived result without an accessible explanation is invalid UX.
- The compact route presentation shows `Route possible` and one total cost value. A small adjacent `?` hover control summarizes the contribution of each factor on demand.
- An impossible route displays `Route impossible` instead of a total cost; its `?` hover lists the concrete blocking capabilities or endpoint constraints.
- `06.4 Physical Station Infrastructure` is retained as an Alpha requirement.
- A Physical Station and an Automated Station are separate entities. A Physical Station is player-accessible infrastructure in a physical gameplay space; an Automated Station is a Star Map-managed Automated Facility without direct physical player access.
- Galaxia Alpha must make at least one Physical Station necessary early in its main progression, but it should not directly prescribe the station's orbit.
- The location should emerge from the player's available destinations and the capability that currently requires the station. Overworld orbit may become the only practical early choice because the Moon is not yet accessible, rather than because the game hardcodes Overworld orbit as the required location.
- The first Moon mission should not require orbital vehicle assembly, refuelling, or similarly complex station logistics; that burden arrives too early in the player's space progression.
- Observation, telescopes, and research are candidate roles for the first required Physical Station, but no exact station capability or output is accepted yet; the remaining decision is routed to Define First Physical Station Contract.
- Working Alpha progression places the first required Physical Station after the player's first Moon activity and before the first Mars mission: `Overworld -> Moon -> Physical Station -> Mars`.
- This ordering is provisional. It preserves the Moon as the simple introduction to space travel, then introduces observation or research infrastructure before Mars alongside the Moon-derived material gate.
- The sequence does not prescribe an orbit. The player may use any eligible location available at that point; the practical choice should emerge from access and station-capability rules.
- Whether the station produces travel-enabling data, performs another kind of research, or serves a different systemic need is intentionally deferred until the surrounding observation and progression systems are clearer.
- The `07.2 Automated Mining` implementation-status claim is substantially accurate: the Miner module is restricted to Automated Outposts, performs resource generation, and deposits retained output into the owning Automated Facility inventory.
- Mining candidates derive from the celestial object's registered ores plus local Planetary Feature effects. Miner focus and ore blacklist/voiding behavior actively affect selection and retained output.
- Accepted Alpha miner controls retain the focus tier, selected focus ore, and blacklist/voiding behavior as meaningful specialization and logistics safeguards.
- Miner parallel capacity should derive from module tier and upgrades rather than act as a freely adjustable operational slider.
- The source's `network demand` direction meant automatically changing miner output according to demand reported by the logistics or facility network. The source defines no demand model or control contract, and current code does not implement this behavior.
- Automatic miner response to `network demand` is rejected from Alpha scope. If a concrete demand system is proposed later, it requires a separate design decision rather than inheriting this source claim.
- `07.1 Planetary Features & Crater Nodes` is routed to Review Crater Nodes Proposal. The owner reopened direct design discussion on 2026-09-16 without waiting for further designer feedback. Existing Planetary Feature effects do not resolve the proposed finite crater-deposit design.
- The `07.3 GregTech Processing & Manufacturing` implementation-status claim is substantially accurate. Automated Facility modules exist for maceration, centrifuging, electrolysis, chemical processing, assembly, and distillation, and they identify concrete GregTech recipe maps.
- Facility recipe selection and NEI-facing recipe interaction are implemented.
- `07.3` requires no separate global product decision during this audit. The existing GregTech integration remains factual, while concrete reasons to perform particular processes off-world belong to later destination- and recipe-specific design.
- `07.4 Advanced Extraction & Alloys` is retained only where supported elsewhere: specialized drilling and fracking remain inside Review Crater Nodes Proposal. Unsupported `space-grade alloy` and orbital-processing claims do not enter the Alpha contract without a concrete higher-priority proposal.
- The `08.1 Supply & Demand Control` implementation-status claim is substantially accurate. Facility logistics expose supply, target, reserve, priority, committed or inbound cargo, and dispatch-status concepts used by current HAMMER planning and upkeep behavior.
- The `08.2 HAMMER Local Delivery` implementation-status claim is substantially accurate: dispatch planning evaluates source surplus, protected reserve, destination capacity, and route availability before creating tracked deliveries. Broader construction and recovery flows are not established by this fact.
- The `08.3 Orbital Deliveries` foundation exists: deliveries carry route and departure state, remain tracked in transit, synchronize to clients, and resolve into destination logistics state after travel.
- `08.4 Advanced Transport Networks` is not accepted as one authoritative roadmap. Its cargo-ship and advanced-freight portions are superseded by Define Alpha Transport Roles and Define Vehicle Capability and Route Requirements. Construction supply, recovery, AE2 boundary integration, and railcannon-scale transport require independent support from higher-priority sources or later tickets before entering the Alpha contract.
- `09.4 Gameplay Mod Integrations` is retained only as an implementation inventory: GT Teams identity, GregTech recipe integration, NEI interaction, and Baubles-based oxygen equipment integration exist. Its reference to configurable team roles is rejected with `09.1`, and its documentation-placement advice creates no product requirement.
