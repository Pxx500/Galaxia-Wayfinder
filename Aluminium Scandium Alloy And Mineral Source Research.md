# Aluminium Scandium Alloy And Mineral Source Research

Source: Verified USGS, European Commission CORDIS, NASA NTRS, APWORKS, Nikon SLM Solutions, and Eplus3D sources linked below
Imported: 2026-10-10

## Source Snapshot

This is research intake, not accepted Galaxia design. It investigates the real-world inspiration behind the preserved Crater Nodes Proposal. It does not select recipes, change accepted atmosphere-free production gameplay, or establish a supported lunar resource model.

### Short Answer

The original aluminium-scandium idea has real inspiration in two independent areas. Al-Sc alloys have aerospace applications. Scandium recovery from bauxite-processing residue is also a real research and pilot-processing subject. These facts do not substantiate scandium-rich lunar bauxite deposits, a rich asteroid scandium resource, the proposal's exact chemistry or quantities, or a universal need to manufacture Al-Sc in the natural vacuum of space.

### Aerospace Alloy Evidence And Addition Scale

The [USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries 2026 scandium chapter](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026-scandium.pdf), dated February 2026 and reporting 2025 activity, identifies aerospace alloys and solid oxide fuel cells as principal scandium uses. The previous [2025 chapter](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2025/mcs2025-scandium.pdf) names aluminium-scandium alloys and solid oxide fuel cells. Both PDFs were retrieved successfully and parsed, including their document titles.

[APWORKS, the Scalmalloy developer](https://www.apworks.de/scalmalloy), reports aerospace applications and components used in environments including space. This is first-party product evidence, not a claim that every spacecraft uses this alloy. Its listed tensile strength, corrosion performance and applications are specific to Scalmalloy and should not be generalized to every Al-Sc composition.

The [Nikon SLM Solutions Scalmalloy Material Data Sheet](https://nikon-slm-solutions.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/mds5143.pdf), version `MDS_Scalmalloy_2024-11.1_EN`, lists a customised powder composition of 0.68–0.84% Sc by weight, 4.50–4.90% Mg, and aluminium as the balance, plus other alloying elements. Its composition note explicitly says mass or weight percent. This is a concrete sub-percent scandium addition in a commercial Al-Mg-Sc-Zr alloy, not a universal formula for all aluminium-scandium alloys.

The [Equispheres AlMgScZr material sheet](https://equispheres.com/wp-content/uploads/equispheres-material-data-sheet-almgsczr-scalmalloy.pdf) independently lists Sc 0.68–0.88 and aluminium as the balance. Nikon's sheet is the clearer source for the explicit weight-percent unit.

The [EU SCALE project report](https://cordis.europa.eu/article/id/436238-elusive-scandium-awaits-in-industrial-by-products) describes small scandium additions as improving aluminium-alloy properties. It also distinguishes scandium compounds, Al-Sc master alloys, and the final high-performance alloy applications. A master alloy is a processing intermediate used to introduce scandium into other aluminium material, not necessarily the final structural composition. USGS 2026 includes a quoted 2% Sc aluminium-alloy ingot product. That market entry is not evidence that finished aerospace components generally contain 2% Sc.

Useful short excerpt from Nikon note 1: “Compositions stated as mass or weight percent.”

### Recovery Sources And The Bauxite Connection

USGS 2026 states that scandium was produced exclusively as a byproduct, primarily from nickel and titanium process streams and previously processed tailings or residues. It describes resources as abundant but rarely highly concentrated. USGS 2025 additionally names cobalt and zirconium streams and historical uranium, iron-ore, rare-earth and apatite sources. These reports support a difficult trace-metal recovery story. They do not support treating scandium as a commonly mined standalone bulk ore.

The EU SCALE report documents pilot extraction from bauxite residue and acid waste from titanium-dioxide pigment production. It describes these as containing trace scandium. Researchers processed residue and waste into scandium concentrates, refined compounds such as Sc2O3 and ScF3, and trialled Al-Sc master-alloy production. Its reported concentrate content of up to 25 wt% Sc refers to processed concentrate, not the incoming bauxite, red mud, a natural orebody or an aerospace alloy.

The [REDMUD project's own final reporting](https://cordis.europa.eu/project/id/636876/reporting) describes bauxite residue as a byproduct of producing alumina from bauxite. It reports selective leaching and further recovery methods for titanium and rare-earth elements including scandium. It also identifies processing costs and intrinsic material value as obstacles to practical industrial adoption. A demonstrated extraction route is not automatically a profitable widespread commercial supply source.

Useful short excerpt from USGS 2026: “Resources of scandium are abundant but rarely occur in high concentrations”.

The preserved proposal's connection between alumina production, red mud and a secondary scandium output is therefore well grounded at the concept level. A detailed real route involves mineral preparation, leaching, separation/refining, and metal or master-alloy production. The current evidence does not validate a sifter directly producing metallic scandium from solution, complete acid recovery, exact sodium-hydroxide balances, or direct oxide-to-final-alloy quantities.

### The Author's Exact Nitric-Acid Source

The recovered original link is [Shoppert et al., Selective Scandium (Sc) Extraction from Bauxite Residue (Red Mud) Obtained by Alkali Fusion-Leaching Method](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8779884/), *Materials* 15(2), 433, 2022. Its PMC copy was fully retrieved and parsed. This is the original experimental paper, not a review.

Its specially prepared alkali-fusion residue contained about 140–150 mg/kg Sc. Preparation fused bauxite with NaOH at 300°C, then water-leached, filtered and washed it. Dilute nitric-acid leaching recovered 71.2% of that scandium at pH 2, 80°C and 90 minutes. Acid was added to maintain pH. Filtration separated a scandium-bearing solution from a predominantly iron-oxide residue.

This directly supports the proposal's red-mud plus dilute-nitric-acid inspiration. It does not demonstrate identical performance for ordinary Bayer residue, a pressure chamber in space, metallic scandium dust obtained by sifting, full nitric-acid regeneration, or the stated NaOH coproduct and numerical yields. Scandium in the solution still needs downstream separation and refining. The paper's extraction percentage is a fraction of trace scandium already present, not a 71.2% scandium ore grade.

### Vacuum Is Not A General Al-Sc Requirement

Nikon's Scalmalloy data sheet explicitly specifies a post-build heat treatment at 325°C in air for four hours. This alone rules out the blanket statement that all processing of the alloy and its components must occur in vacuum.

The [Eplus3D report of its Airbus Central Research & Technology collaboration](https://www.eplus3d.com/eplus3d-collaborates-with-airbus-central-research-technology-for-scalmalloy-optimization-in-mpbf.html), dated 12 November 2024, describes Scalmalloy samples made on a terrestrial EP-M260 laser powder-bed-fusion machine. It uses a low-oxygen process environment prepared by hybrid purging assisted by a vacuum pump. Controlled atmosphere or vacuum-assisted equipment is not the same thing as requiring a naturally atmosphere-free destination. The report also describes ageing and hot-isostatic-pressing treatments.

Useful short excerpt from Nikon note 9: “heated to 325 °C, in air”.

These sources do not establish that vacuum is never useful in alloy processing. They establish that a natural space vacuum is not a general prerequisite for this alloy family. Galaxia's accepted atmosphere-free production rule can remain a gameplay rule without being presented as a demonstrated universal metallurgical requirement.

### Lunar And Asteroid Claims

The [NASA NTRS record for Pavlenko, Simonova and Karyakin, 1974](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/search.jsp?R=19750003769) describes measured trace scandium in Luna 16 regolith and comparisons with Apollo lunar samples. The record has a readable abstract but no available full-text download. It supports scandium's presence in sampled lunar material, not commercially exploitable scandium deposits or crater-specific enrichment.

The [NASA NTRS record for Schmitt and Smith, 1963](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/search.jsp?R=19640009687) describes neutron-activation analysis of scandium and other elements in meteorites and individual chondrules. Again, the accessible evidence is the research record and abstract rather than a recovered full report. It supports measured meteoritic scandium, not a quantified mineable asteroid resource. Meteorite presence is not proof that a selected asteroid has a recoverable concentrated deposit.

The [USGS descriptive model of laterite-type bauxite deposits](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/b1693/html/bull21ut.htm) describes bauxite as weathered residual material and its depositional environment as surface weathering in warm to hot, wet climates. Intensive weathering is part of that deposit model. This is a terrestrial geological process, not evidence for natural lunar bauxite. It does not prove every imaginable bauxite-bearing extraterrestrial fragment impossible, but a lunar crater full of scandium-rich bauxite needs fictional or separately substantiated provenance.

No checked source in this bounded pass establishes lunar or asteroid scandium-rich bauxite, economically exploitable extraterrestrial Sc deposits, an impact mechanism that produces such enrichment, or the proposal's `10 Al2O3:2 Sc` composition. Those are unsupported by this research, not proven impossible. Detectable abundance, local concentration, recoverability and economic viability are different claims.

The proposal's dust-unit ratios should not be compared directly with industrial weight percentages unless a physical mass convention is explicitly defined. They are game quantities. A sub-percent real alloy addition can inspire a recipe without forcing literal realistic yield or balance.

The resolved proposal-review note already accepts one canonical Space-Grade Alloy item and atmosphere-free production as gameplay direction. This research does not reopen those decisions. ### Lore Implications, Inferences Only

- A bulk aluminium-rich feedstock with a small, difficult-to-recover scandium coproduct has a strong real-world analogue
- A residue-recovery chain could provide a reason to process large amounts of feedstock for a valuable small alloying output
- A high processed-concentrate grade should not be used as the natural deposit grade
- An aluminium-bearing lunar silicate or explicitly fictional enriched impact deposit is more defensible as lore than unexplained lunar bauxite. This is an inference from the terrestrial bauxite model, not a selected material definition
- A naturally atmosphere-free production requirement should be explained as Galaxia's authored process or gameplay constraint, not a universal rule of Al-Sc metallurgy
- “Space-grade” can mean a fictional engineering material inspired by real light, strong alloys. It need not claim that aluminium-scandium, gold-cobalt and iron-platinum are scientifically identical substitutes
